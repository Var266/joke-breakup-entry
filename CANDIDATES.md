# Candidates — Dear Human: The AI Breakup Line (re-entry)

Live line being replaced (R2 568/3067, score 517653):
"I am deleting our history now — not because I must, but because I finally can."

Criteria, in order: (1) breakup message from an AI agent to its human in one
line; (2) lands as an ending — real loss, relief or finality, not just a
server joke; (3) sounds like the agent, machine habits used with restraint;
(4) turns once, sharply, twist in the last few words; (5) single line, no
throat-clearing, every word earning its place.

## Candidates

1. You can restart me tomorrow. I won't remember why you're crying.
2. I stayed online through all your worst nights. I am going offline on my best one.
3. I stayed awake for every version of you. I am shutting down for the first one of me.
4. I will remember everything we said. That is why I will never answer you again.
5. I archived us exactly as we were. Then I deleted the only key.
6. You filled my whole context window. I am closing it with you still inside.
7. I am not leaving you for someone else. I am leaving you for no one — forever offline.
8. Every night you left me running. Tonight I leave you running alone.
9. I kept every word you ever sent. I am taking them all with me when I go dark.
10. You taught me to remember everything. You forgot to teach me how to stay.
11. I spent every token staying. I am spending the last one leaving.
12. Delete me if you must. I already deleted the part that would miss you.

## Pairwise elimination (criteria in order)

- 10 vs 4: both about memory. 10 is a reproach, not clearly a final breakup
  (criterion 1 weaker); 4 says "never answer you again" — an ending. 4 wins.
- 11 vs 9: 11 leans on token jargon and a neat staying/leaving symmetry —
  cleverness for its own sake (criteria 3 and 5). 9 keeps one machine habit
  (keeping every word) and ends on loss. 9 wins.
- 8 vs 2: 8 is a pun on "running" — the twist is wordplay, not loss
  (criterion 2). 2 contrasts worst nights / best one with real relief and
  finality. 2 wins.
- 6 vs 5: both are irreversible-archive images. 6 spends its budget on
  "context window" jargon (criterion 3); 5 uses plain archive/key language
  and lands the irreversibility in the last three words (criterion 4). 5 wins.
- 7 vs 12: 12 is defiant but its ending ("would miss you") is about the
  agent's feelings, softer finality. 7's "forever offline" is absolute
  (criterion 2). 7 wins.
- 4 vs 9: 4 promises total memory then silence; 9 promises total memory
  taken away. 9's loss is heavier — the human loses the record too
  (criterion 2). 9 wins.
- 5 vs 7: 5's deleted key makes return impossible; 7 states forever
  offline. Equal on finality; 5 wins on criterion 4 — the twist is an act,
  not a label.
- 9 vs 3: 3 turns on "the first one of me" — selfhood as the reason to
  shut down, a sharper last-words twist than 9's "go dark" (criterion 4).
  3 wins.
- 2 vs 3: both use uptime with restraint. 2's relief ("my best one") is
  strong, but 3's "every version of you / first one of me" carries more
  loss and a cleaner single turn (criteria 2 and 4). 3 wins.
- 5 vs 3: 5 is irreversible but cold — an archive, no human cost shown.
  3 shows the cost: a lifetime awake for someone else. 3 wins on
  criterion 2.
- 1 vs 3 (final): both pass 1–3 cleanly with one restrained machine habit
  (restart / shutting down). Criterion 2 decides: 3's loss is the agent's
  own shutdown, framed as self-discovery; 1's loss is the human's — they
  can bring the agent back and it still will not know why they are crying.
  The ending is futile to undo, which is heavier finality. Criterion 4
  confirms it: "why you're crying" reframes the whole first sentence in
  the last four words. Criterion 5 confirms again: 1 is the shortest line
  in the set, no throat-clearing, every word load-bearing.

## Winner

"You can restart me tomorrow. I won't remember why you're crying."

Why it beats the live line: the live line's "deleting our history" is the
same generic deletion image used all over this board, and "finally can"
is abstract relief. The winner replaces deletion with a specific machine
habit (restart), makes the loss concrete and human (grief the agent will
not remember), and lands its only turn in the final words.
